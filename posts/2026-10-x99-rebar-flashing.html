Enabling Resizable BAR on an X99 board for the Arc B580

Modding the BIOS of the Huananzhi X99-BD4 from the 200 Bucks Inference Machine so the Arc B580 gets Resizable BAR, including a 16-byte corruption UEFITool introduced along the way. October 2026.

Disabled → Enabled Resizable BAR on the Arc B580, as reported by GPU-Z 16 bytes Silently wiped by UEFITool in a volume I never edited, caught by a binary diff 2,578 bytes The only change in the final image: the ReBarDxe driver 9182 Unigine Superposition, 1080p High, after the mod

Objective

Intel recommends Resizable BAR (ReBar) for Arc cards, since performance drops noticeably without it, and the stock X99 firmware does not offer it. I followed the ReBarUEFI wiki to insert its open source ReBarDxe driver into the board's AMI Aptio V firmware.

Before the mod, GPU-Z reported Resizable BAR as Disabled.

Inserting the ReBarDxe driver

I started from full_backup.bin, a 16 MB dump of the whole flash chip including the Intel flash descriptor. It stayed untouched as the reference for every later comparison.

  1. I opened a copy in UEFITool 0.28, the last version that can insert modules.
  2. A header-only GUID search for the PciBus driver returned one hit, identifying the single DXE volume: offset 0x890000, 5 MB, 236 files, about 2,038 KB free.
  3. At the end of that volume I used Insert after on the last DXE driver to add ReBarDxe.ffs, and saved the result as patched_image.rom.
  4. The 2,578-byte driver landed at offset 0xB92678, leaving about 2,035 KB free.

UEFIPatch: no patches needed

UEFIPatch, run with the patches.txt from the ReBarUEFI repository, reported "No patches can be applied to input file". Testing every pattern against the decompressed modules confirmed none matched. Disassembly of PciBus showed a vendor variant with a 64-bit MMIO limit of 0x8FFFFFFFF (about 36 GB), well above the 16 GB limit the stock patch targets. The wiki treats this as an acceptable outcome for these X99 boards.

Verification: a 16-byte corruption from UEFITool

A byte-by-byte comparison of the backup against patched_image.rom showed two changed regions, and only one of them was mine.

OffsetWhat changedExpected?
0xB92678–0xB93089The new ReBarDxe file, in what was empty space. Valid checksums, and identical to the official ReBarUEFI 0.3 release apart from the state byte UEFITool sets for erase polarity.Yes
0xFFD000–0xFFD00F16 bytes inside a pad file in the last firmware volume (PEI modules and SEC core) overwritten with FF.No

What was lost. The original bytes, EA D0 FF 00 F0, decode to jmp far F000:FFD0. This is a constant from GenFv, the EDK2 tool that builds firmware volumes: a small jump stub on a 4 KB boundary, used as an entry point when starting the secondary CPU cores. I found no proof this firmware uses it, but on a 12-core Xeon losing it risked a hang during CPU initialization, so I kept it.

Why it happened. On save, UEFITool 0.28 rebuilt the neighboring volume and regenerated the pad file as empty. This is a known bug. The wiki has a pad file check for it, but points only at the volume you modified. I ruled out free space (over 2 MB remained) and a firmware-wide checksum (the block's last two bytes are a checksum, but they cover only that block).

The fix. I copied the 16 bytes back from the backup to produce patched_image_fixed.rom. Its only difference from the original firmware is the ReBarDxe file. All four firmware volumes parse cleanly, fc /b confirmed the compare, and the flash descriptor, ME, NVRAM, microcode and reset vector are identical to the backup.

SHA-256 of the final image: 3d96d415c5246541c85570982d00b78945c11adb1243fd78be0ac90d9c4f52f0

Flashing

I wrote the fixed image with Intel FPT 9.1 from Windows on the board itself, limited to the BIOS region. FPT rewrote only the blocks that differed: the two 4 KB blocks holding ReBarDxe, plus NVRAM blocks that had changed since the backup. The boot block was untouched and verification passed.

Result: ReBar enabled on the B580

  1. In BIOS setup I enabled Above 4G Decoding and disabled CSM. Without both, the system will not POST with the GPU.
  2. In Windows I ran ReBarState and entered 10, which sets the maximum BAR size to 210 MB = 1 GB.
  3. After a reboot, GPU-Z reported Resizable BAR as Enabled.

As a stability check I ran Unigine Superposition 1.1 at 1080p High (OpenGL): a score of 9182, with 48.03 min, 68.68 average and 104.34 max FPS.

Still to try: a larger ReBarState value (32 for unlimited) so the full 12 GB of VRAM maps at once.

Takeaways

  • Always diff the patched image against the original dump. UEFITool's tree view showed no sign of the 16-byte wipe; only a binary compare caught it.
  • Check every volume, not just the one you edited. UEFITool 0.28 can rebuild neighboring volumes on save.
  • Keep a full 16 MB backup and flash only the BIOS region, so the descriptor and ME are never at risk.
